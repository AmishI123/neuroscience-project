===== QUESTION 1: Why are you applying to ASDRP? (373 of 375 words) =====
At Pragnya, where I volunteer with neurodivergent children, I once watched one child dance while another covered their ears because the room was too loud. It was the same room and the same noise, but the two kids reacted in opposite ways. The difference had to be inside each child, in how their brain was taking it all in. I still think about that day.
It made me curious about why the same thing can feel so different depending on what is going on inside someone's brain. That curiosity is what got me into neuroscience, and so far I have explored it through code. In my main project, I used public recordings of thousands of neurons in mouse visual cortex to see whether a mouse's pupil size and running could help predict what its neurons were doing, beyond the image it was looking at. They did, in all three mice I analyzed. Then I shuffled the behavior data to see if the result would fall apart, and the improvement went away. Honestly, that check convinced me more than the result did.
Still, predicting activity only tells me so much. I want to understand what is actually changing inside the neurons, and that is part of why I plan to become a Certified Registered Nurse Anesthetist. Anesthesia turns consciousness off and back on on purpose, and I am curious what happens to neurons and the signals between them when it does, and how drugs and diseases change those signals.
I am applying to ASDRP because I have taken my independent work about as far as I can on my own. I want a mentor who can teach me to analyze data more carefully, push back on my own results, and explain what I find. I also want real lab experience, since a lot of what I am curious about cannot be figured out from a laptop. I also want to work with students who get me into things I would never find by myself.
In the long run, I want to keep combining neuroscience and healthcare through college and beyond, so that understanding the brain at the cellular level helps me make better decisions for patients. ASDRP feels like the right place to start.

===== QUESTION 5: Hands-on science or research experience (374 of 375 words) =====
I have not been part of a formal lab program yet, but I have done computational research using public biological datasets. Each project taught me something different about when to trust a result.
My main project used public recordings from thousands of neurons in mouse visual cortex. I did most of the work myself, with advice and guidance from my father. I wanted to know whether a mouse's behavior, meaning its pupil size and running, could help predict its brain activity better than the image it was looking at. Adding behavior raised my model's accuracy on held-out data from 0.126 to 0.158 in the first mouse, and the result held up in two more mice (0.154 to 0.203, and 0.143 to 0.191). Pupil size helped in all three. Running barely helped in the mouse that hardly ran, but it did help in the two that ran more. After that, I tried to break the result. When I shuffled the behavior data, the improvement disappeared, and it stayed positive when I left out any one mouse. This project taught me to test my own results before believing them. It also showed me its limits, since it shows prediction and not cause, and three mice are not enough to generalize.
Another project used a brain imaging dataset. I built logistic regression and random forest models to tell healthy scans apart from Alzheimer's scans. The random forest did much better, which told me the patterns were more complicated than a simple straight-line boundary could capture.
I also looked at a sleep health dataset to see whether common claims about stress and steps held up. I trained a linear regression and a decision tree to predict sleep duration. Stress and age both related to sleep, but age was also related to stress, so I read about confounding variables before trusting either one.
These projects only let me look for patterns in data. To understand what is actually happening inside neurons, like why memory breaks down in Alzheimer's, I need to work with real cells in a lab, which is why molecular neuroscience interests me. At ASDRP I want lab experience and mentorship to build on the computational skills I have. My code, results and apps are at amishi123.github.io/neuroscience-project.
